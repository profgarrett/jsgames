observational study, a research method where researchers observe and analyze existing data without manipulating variables or conditions
treatment, an intervention, action, or condition applied to subjects in a study to measure its effect on outcomes
outcome, the result or dependent variable that researchers measure to assess the impact of treatments or interventions
association, a statistical relationship between two or more variables that may or may not indicate causation
causal association, a relationship where one variable directly causes changes in another variable
causality, the principle that one event or factor directly produces or influences another event or outcome
comparison, the process of examining differences between groups, conditions, or time periods to identify patterns or effects
treatment group, the group of subjects in an experiment that receives the intervention being tested
control group, the group of subjects in an experiment that does not receive the treatment, serving as a baseline for comparison
epidemiology, the study of patterns, causes, and effects of health and disease conditions in populations
confounding, when an unmeasured third variable influences both the treatment and outcome, creating a false association
randomization, the process of randomly assigning subjects to different groups to eliminate bias and ensure fair comparison
randomized controlled experiment, a study where subjects are randomly assigned to treatment and control groups to test causal relationships
randomized controlled trial (RCT), a specific type of randomized experiment, often used in medical and business research to test interventions
blind, a study design where participants (and sometimes researchers) don't know which group they're in to prevent bias
placebo, an inactive treatment given to the control group that appears identical to the real treatment being tested
